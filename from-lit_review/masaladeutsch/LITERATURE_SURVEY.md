# Literature survey: what the blog covers, what the journals cover, and the gap between them

**Date:** 22 August 2026 · **Corpus:** 192 published posts, 32 topic labels ·
**Source estate:** 67 ICAR open-access journals plus the non-ICAR titles already
cited in published work.

Prompted by [IJAgS 45210, "Biofuels production: a sustainable solution to combat
climate change"](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJAgS/article/view/45210) —
an article squarely inside the blog's existing biofuels beat, which raised the
question of what *else* sits in that estate that the blog has never touched.

---

## 1. Method

Three passes, deliberately mechanical so the conclusion is not just an impression:

1. **Corpus profile.** Every post title in `meta/manifest.json` scored against a
   20-theme keyword map. Word-boundary matching, because a first pass scored
   "pulses/legumes" at 10 hits — all false, `gram` matching inside *programme*
   and *kilogram*. Corrected count: zero.
2. **Source estate.** Enumerated all 67 journals on ICAR's `epubs` platform, then
   pulled current tables of contents from the five closest to the blog's subject
   matter, plus five back-issues of the closest single title.
3. **Cross-reference.** Each recurring journal theme checked against the blog
   corpus for prior coverage, so nothing below is a duplicate of published work.

**Deepest sample:** *Agricultural Economics Research Review* (AERR), 47 titles
across five issues. AERR is the closest match in the estate to how this blog
already argues — comparative advantage, price transmission, impact evaluation,
public investment — and is the highest-yield source for future pieces.

## 2. What the blog is: a downstream publication

Theme hits across 192 post titles:

| Band | Themes | Hits |
|---|---|---|
| **Heavy** | trade & tariffs | 63 |
| | logistics & storage | 54 |
| | macro & prices | 33 |
| | ethanol & biofuels | 26 |
| | oil & refining | 26 |
| **Medium** | crops & agronomy 14 · chemicals 13 · credit & finance 12 · climate & carbon 10 | |
| **Thin** | gas & LNG 8 · fertiliser 7 · power 7 · dairy & livestock 3 · water 2 | |
| **Absent** | labour · seeds & genetics · extension & adoption · horticulture | 0 |

The pattern is consistent enough to name. **This is a downstream publication.**
It is strong on what happens to a commodity *after* it leaves the farm gate —
traded, tariffed, refined, blended, stored, priced — and close to silent on what
happens before it. Where the blog does go upstream it goes as far as the input
(urea, diesel, FOM) and stops at the field boundary. It has never written about
the people, land, water or germplasm on the other side of that boundary.

That is a coherent editorial identity, not a failing. But it means an entire
literature — the one ICAR actually publishes — sits unused.

## 4. Commissioning list

Ranked by fit with the blog's existing method — data-led, sourced to primary
statistics, with a counterintuitive finding — rather than by subject novelty.

### Tier 1 — strongest fit, closest to what already works

**1. What a farm labourer earns, and why it stopped tracking output**
AERR's wages paper is the single largest gap in the corpus: 63 posts on trade
and not one on farm labour. Pair the AERR series with Labour Bureau rural wage
data and the blog's existing CPI work. The natural hook is real versus nominal
wages against food inflation — the blog already has the CPI machinery built.

**2. India has enough ethanol targets. Does it have the feedstock?**
AERR asks this directly ("Does India have enough feedstock to meet its E20
targets by 2025?"). The blog has published on maize acreage, sugarcane diversion
and the ethanol buyers' market — this closes the loop with a supply-side
balance. Highest continuity with published work of anything on this list.

**3. The 4:2:1 sequel: nano-urea and whether it substitutes for anything**
The urea piece established the price and dosing argument. IJAgS is currently
full of nano-nitrogen trials, and IFFCO's nano-urea has a large public
deployment with contested agronomic results. A piece testing the marketing claim
against the trial literature is directly in the blog's register.

**4. Market integration: does a tomato price in Kolar reach Delhi?**
AERR on tomato market integration and e-NAM performance in Odisha. The blog has
mapped CPI by state; this is the mechanism underneath it. Agmarknet gives daily
mandi prices, so it is fully reproducible.

### Tier 2 — new ground, method transfers cleanly

**5. Who owns a variety? Plant variety rights and the seed-IP question**
AERR on essentially derived varieties under PPVFR. Connects to the blog's
existing import-substitution and IPR interests, and nothing on the blog touches
agricultural IP.

**6. Irrigation is a public investment with a recovery rate. What is it?**
AERR on financial recovery and governance of state irrigation investment. Water
has two mentions in 192 posts, and this is the fiscal framing the blog is good at.

**7. Contract farming after the farm laws: what the evidence actually shows**
AERR has both an Indian assessment and a Nepal comparison. Politically loaded,
which is precisely why an evidence-led treatment has value.

**8. Soil organic carbon: the number under every fertiliser argument**
JISSS is publishing heavily on SOC. The urea and FOM pieces both gesture at
organic carbon without ever measuring it. This is the missing quantitative base.

### Tier 3 — worthwhile, further from the core

**9.** Livestock as a poverty-reduction channel (AERR) — the blog's dairy and
animal-protein pieces are export-facing; this is the domestic-income side.
**10.** Crop insurance: PMFBY claims-to-premium ratios by state.
**11.** Farm mechanisation and the custom-hiring economy (JAE, entirely unused).
**12.** Fisheries beyond exports — marine livelihoods and insurance (AERR, IJF).
**13.** Millets after the International Year of Millets: procurement versus
consumption.
**14.** Agritech landscape and whether ICT adoption changes production decisions
(AERR has both) — pairs with the blog's AI Tracker.

## 5. Non-ICAR sources worth adding to the rotation

Already used in published work and under-mined:

- **Economic & Political Weekly** — cited in the urea piece via the Chand and
  Pavithra paper; the archive is deep on exactly the upstream questions above.
- **NITI Aayog working papers** — host of that same paper; publishes on
  irrigation, fertiliser and agricultural markets.
- **Indian Journal of Agricultural Economics** (ISAE) — the other major
  agricultural-economics title, not on the ICAR platform.
- **Oxford Institute for Energy Studies** — already used for European gas; also
  publishes on Indian gas and LNG.
- **ACER, IEEFA, IGU** — already used; keep for the energy beat.

---

## 6. Caveats

- Coverage was scored on **post titles only**, not full text. A theme discussed
  inside a piece but absent from its title will read as a gap here. The zero
  counts are reliable at title level; treat the medium bands as approximate.
- The ICAR sample is **five journals deep out of 67**, chosen for subject
  proximity. The veterinary, fisheries and horticulture titles were enumerated
  but not read; there is more in them than section 4 reflects.
- AERR's most recent listed issue is **Vol. 37 No. 1 (2024)** — the platform is
  not fully current, so anything time-sensitive needs a check against a newer
  source before publication.
- Nothing here has been fact-checked to the standard of a published post. These
  are commissioning leads, and each would need its primary data located and
  verified in the usual way before writing.
